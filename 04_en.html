Chapter 4. Shadow on the Streets

 

Iris stood by the panoramic window, her hands clasped behind her back. Beyond the glass, the scattered lights of the capital pulsed with an erratic rhythm, like the nervous system of a colossal organism.   

Her gaze was focused yet detached, as if she were seeing not just the city, but its hidden veins: the flows of people, data, and influence, all ready to be reshaped to her will.

The door opened soundlessly.

“You summoned me, Ms. Tanaka?” a smooth, measured voice asked.

“Come in, Friedrich,” she said without turning.

Steiner entered and stopped two paces behind her, his movements economical and precise. Tall, he was a man trusted with secrets no one else was meant to hear. She relied on him.

“I need more control,” she said calmly. “We stand at a turning point. The economy already runs through us. The next step is the political fabric. It has worn thin, like old skin, and it is time to shed it.”

Steiner remained silent, absorbing every word.

“We begin with the capital. From here, we move to the major cities. I want the people’s habit of obedience to shift from the state to us. Without resistance. Without noise. They should wake up one morning and find themselves living within a new system, without even noticing the old one is gone.”

Steiner missed nothing. At last—something worthy of him.

“And the first step,” Iris continued, “must be the integration of our security apparatus into the very structure of society. It must be done in such a way that no one doubts its necessity—that they believe it is the only way out.”

She turned to face him. “Your proposals?”

He gave a short, military-style nod. A fire was rising in him. Finally, a leader had emerged who was not afraid of difficult decisions.

“We have significant influence over the Eastern and Southern sectors,” Steiner began. “Several local factions already depend on us—funding, logistics, weaponry. Our special department has embedded agents to coordinate them; we hold the reins. We can carefully initiate a chain of skirmishes. Territorial conflicts first, then framed as politically motivated. It must look spontaneous.”

“How manageable is it?”

“In the first stage—completely. Beyond that, deviations are possible. But our coordinators will allow us to guide the escalation and intensify key incidents as needed.”

“The targets?”

“Zones where your units will be required to appear. We create the sense of a threat—and then provide the answer in the form of Kairo’s security force. Only they will seem competent.”

Iris nodded thoughtfully. The clarity of it appealed to her.

“Create fear. Then offer structure as the solution. People always gravitate toward order, even if it is provided by the very one who engineered the chaos.”

“Exactly, Ms. Tanaka,” Steiner confirmed with a predatory smile. He could feel they were on the same wavelength.

“We can also provoke clashes between certain gangs in the north,” Steiner continued. “I have several trusted contractors. Professionals. They’ve handled a number of overseas assignments for us. They could easily attack one group while disguised as another. Retaliation will follow. Chaos will ensue. The city police lack motivation; they don’t have the manpower to handle the unrest. Their budget has been gutted for years. The municipal authorities will be forced to beg for help. The corporation steps in as the city’s savior.”

He paused, letting the vision settle. “After the official request, we conduct a sweep. Against the chaos unleashed, we will appear as the guarantors of stability.”

“Provoking isn’t the same as ruling,” Iris clarified. “Their actions must appear to originate from them, not us. So that no one suspects a thing.”

“Of course. We set the pretext. Provide the catalyst. After that, instinct will do the rest.”

Steiner was drawn to the scale of it. He wondered whether she understood the full risk.

“Perhaps, at a strategic moment, we should engineer a few incidents at our own facilities,” he suggested. “To demonstrate that only Kairo has the strength to withstand the chaos. It must be obvious to everyone who they should turn to for help.”

Iris walked slowly around the room, studying Steiner. She saw in him not a pawn, but a partner in a dance of shadows.

“I will issue a statement,” she said. “First, as a concerned observer. Then, as the city’s partner. And in the finale—as the only force capable of restoring order. Exosuits, drones, neural networks. Everything will be introduced under the guise of temporary assistance.”

“The corporation can easily transition building security into street patrols,” Steiner confirmed. “And integrate them into the city-wide surveillance network. That way, we get the full picture. Not just of the streets, but of their collective pulse. Their reactions. Their moods.”

Iris approached the console and activated the panel. A model of the metropolis appeared on the screen. Sectors. Points of influence. Data flows.

“And what will the chief of security say to this?” Steiner asked. He did not want a third party interfering.

“He will say what I tell him to say. Begin today. I want the first incidents by morning. Nothing major. Just... unsettling. Enough for the news feeds to react. Let society feel the tension, like a crack in the foundation.”

Steiner inclined his head slightly, a shadow of satisfaction on his face.

“Consider it already begun.”

He turned to leave.

“Friedrich,” she called out.

He stopped.

“You understand how dangerous this game is. That is exactly why it is worth it. We won't get a second chance. Everything must look like the natural course of events. No one must guess that we are pulling the strings of chaos. In this game, chaos serves order. The right order.”
